### Homepage introduction

I’m Lucas Fournier, a PhD researcher in the ISID team at Cnam’s CEDRIC laboratory. My research explores interpretable rule extraction from historical knowledge graphs, with a focus on governance and careers at the medieval University of Paris.

### Short biography

Lucas Fournier is a PhD researcher at the Conservatoire national des arts et métiers (Cnam), in the ISID team of the CEDRIC laboratory. He began his PhD in October 2026. His research interests connect knowledge graphs, neuro-symbolic AI and digital humanities, with particular attention to uncertainty and the interpretation of historical evidence.

### Extended biography

I began my PhD at Cnam on 1 October 2026 and belong to the ISID team at the CEDRIC laboratory. My thesis director is Cédric du Mouza, with supervision from Stéphane Lamassé and Camelia Constantin.

My initial thesis topic explores how neural and symbolic methods might help extract interpretable rules about governance and careers at the medieval University of Paris. A central challenge is to assess what such rules mean when the underlying historical evidence is incomplete, ambiguous or contradictory. I am interested in connecting computational patterns with explanations that historians can assess in their documentary and historical context.

### Research overview

Historical knowledge graphs connect people, institutions, roles and events recorded in documentary sources. My initial thesis proposal asks how to discover explicit or latent rules in these graphs and assess their confidence while accounting for uncertain identities, imprecise dates and missing evidence.

The proposed historical focus includes career trajectories, access to offices, co-optation and relationships between university membership and ecclesiastical positions. These are topics for investigation rather than established findings of my research.

The research direction and methods remain under development.

## Initial thesis title

**English translation:** Neuro-symbolic rule extraction for governance and careers at the medieval University of Paris from a prosopographical knowledge graph.

This is the initial thesis title; the eventual research direction is still developing.

## Proposed methods

These methods appear in the initial thesis proposal. They are candidate approaches, not an adopted architecture, completed implementation or demonstrated result.

- **Neural candidate generation:** graph embeddings and graph neural networks to explore patterns, suggest missing relations or generate candidate rules.
- **Symbolic rule extraction:** inductive logic programming and Markov logic networks as possible ways to formalize and assess candidate rules.
- **Historical assessment:** historian feedback to examine the meaning, documentary support and exceptions of proposed rules.
- **Uncertainty and time:** contextual confidence assessments that consider source quality, estimated completeness and imprecise temporal information.
- **Textual evidence:** language models are mentioned as a possible means of exploiting source texts; their use is not established.
